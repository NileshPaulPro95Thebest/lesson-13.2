CRICKET

Cricket is a bat-and-ball team sport played between two teams of eleven players on a large oval field. Originating in 16th-century southeast England, it expanded globally through the British Empire and is now governed internationally by the International Cricket Council (ICC).

The Layout and Equipment-


The game centers on a rectangular 22-yard pitch located in the middle of the field. At each end of the pitch stand three wooden stumps known as wickets. Players use a wooden bat and a hard, leather-stitched ball. Traditional Test matches feature players in all-white clothing, while limited-overs formats utilize colored team kits.

How the Game is Played-


A match begins with a coin toss to decide which team bats or bowls first.

• Batting: Two batsmen from the batting side stand at opposite ends of the pitch. Their goal is to score as many runs as possible by hitting the ball and running between the wickets or hitting the ball across the boundary line.

• Bowling and Fielding: The fielding team tries to restrict the scoring and dismiss the batsmen. A bowler delivers sets of six legal balls called an "over", aiming to hit the wickets or have a fielder catch the ball before it touches the ground.

Main Formats of Cricket

Cricket is played in three primary international formats:

• Test Cricket: The oldest and longest format, lasting up to five days with 90 overs per day.

• One Day Internationals (ODIs): A limited-overs format where each side plays a single innings of 50 overs.

• Twenty20 (T20): A fast-paced, high-energy format restricted to 20 overs per side, popularized further by massive domestic leagues like the Indian Premier League (IPL).

Impact and Values

In countries like India, Pakistan, and England, cricket is more than just a sport—it is a cultural emotion. Icons like Sachin Tendulkar, Virat Kohli, and MS Dhoni have inspired millions. Beyond entertainment, playing cricket teaches valuable life skills including physical fitness, patience, discipline, and strong teamwork.
